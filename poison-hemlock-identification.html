Poison hemlock identification, compared with wild fennel.

Poison hemlock and wild fennel can sit in the same local learner's mind because the deck calls out the look-alike risk directly. This page turns those deck notes into recognition practice, with no eating, touching, removal, or harvesting advice.

What the deck says to compare

The scent row is deck context, not a handling instruction. Learn the contrast here, then practice from the species pages and quiz.

Field mark Poison Hemlock Wild Fennel
Deck label Do not eat. Recognition only. Edible entry in the deck, but not eating permission.
Stems Smooth hairless stems with purple/maroon blotches Solid green stems.
Leaves Fern-like leaves. Feathery thread-like leaves.
Flowers White flower umbels. Yellow flower umbels.
Scent note Musty or mousy, with no anise scent. Strong anise or licorice smell when crushed. Do not crush or handle plants to test this.
Where the deck places it The plant that killed Socrates, and it's common in damp, disturbed Bay Area ground. A Mediterranean escapee that lines East Bay trailheads and BART tracks.

Do not eat entry

Poison Hemlock

DEADLY. Mistaken for fennel or wild carrot — the purple-blotched stems and lack of anise scent give it away. All parts are lethal.

Use note is context only

Wild Fennel

Resembles DEADLY poison hemlock — but hemlock has purple-blotched stems and a musty smell, never anise. If it doesn't smell of licorice, walk away.
