How to use this page

Use it as a photo-practice checklist

Each card gives the current deck's recognition cues and then points to the full species page and in-app photo guide. Stop at learning the shape, leaf, flower, or growth habit.

What this page does

It helps beginners compare common weedy plants that show up around Berkeley paths, lawns, lots, gardens, and winter-spring hillsides.

What it does not do

It does not tell you what to eat, touch, pull, spray, harvest, or prepare. Forage Berkeley stays a learning tool.

4 deck entries

Sidewalk, lawn, and compacted-edge weeds

These are common low-growing entries learners may notice in parking strips, lawns, paths, and other disturbed ground.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Dandelion

Taraxacum officinale

Season
Year-round; best young in spring
How to recognize it
Basal rosette of jagged 'lion's tooth' leaves, single hollow stem with milky sap, yellow composite flower, puffball seed head.
Recognition boundary
The deck mentions sprayed lawns, roadsides, and yellow composite look-alikes. Use the rosette, hollow stem, milky sap, and puffball as photo-practice cues only.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Common Mallow (Cheeseweed)

Malva neglecta

Season
Spring-fall
How to recognize it
Round scalloped leaves with a crinkled edge, small pale 5-petal flowers, flat 'cheese-wheel' seed pods.
Recognition boundary
The deck flags contaminated soil as the caution context. Practice the round leaves, pale flowers, and cheese-wheel seed pods without treating this as use advice.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Broadleaf Plantain

Plantago major

Season
Spring-fall
How to recognize it
Basal rosette of broad oval leaves with prominent parallel veins; tall slender seed spikes.
Recognition boundary
The deck points to the rosette, parallel veins, and seed spikes. This page keeps those as study cues, not clearance to handle or use a real plant.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Common Sow Thistle

Sonchus oleraceus

Season
Year-round
How to recognize it
Soft, jagged leaves clasping a hollow stem, milky sap, small dandelion-like yellow flowers.
Recognition boundary
The deck contrasts the soft prickles with dandelion relatives. Use the hollow stem, clasping leaves, and yellow flowers for recognition practice.

4 deck entries

Garden-bed and open-lot weeds

These fast-growing entries are useful practice because they are common in disturbed soil and several have toxic or caution look-alike context in the deck.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Chickweed

Stellaria media

Season
Cool season; fall through spring
How to recognize it
Low sprawling mats, small opposite egg-shaped leaves, tiny white star flowers, single line of hairs running along the stem.
Recognition boundary
The deck says to confirm the single hairline and star flower to avoid spurges with milky sap. Treat that as photo-practice context, not a field decision.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Common Purslane

Portulaca oleracea

Season
Warm season; summer
How to recognize it
Fleshy paddle-shaped leaves, smooth reddish stems, low spreading mat, tiny yellow flowers.
Recognition boundary
The deck compares it with toxic prostrate spurge. Keep the thick leaves, reddish stems, and no-milky-sap contrast as recognition cues only.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Lamb's Quarters

Chenopodium album

Season
Spring-summer
How to recognize it
Diamond/goosefoot-shaped leaves with a white mealy coating on new growth, upright stem often striped pink.
Recognition boundary
The deck mentions oxalates and toxic nightshade seedlings. Use the mealy new growth and goosefoot-shaped leaves for learning, not for real-world use.

Use with care / Introduced / Herb

Prickly Lettuce

Lactuca serriola

Season
Spring young growth
How to recognize it
Leaves with a row of prickles down the underside midrib, milky sap, leaves often twisting to face vertically.
Recognition boundary
The deck flags bitterness and mild sedative context when mature. This guide only uses the prickly midrib, milky sap, and upright leaf habit as visual cues.

4 deck entries

Winter and spring hillside weeds

These are seasonal entries learners may notice when Berkeley and East Bay hillsides, paths, and lots turn green and yellow.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Shepherd's Purse

Capsella bursa-pastoris

Season
Cool season
How to recognize it
Basal rosette of toothed leaves; thin stalk of distinctive heart/purse-shaped seed pods.
Recognition boundary
The deck calls the purse-shaped pods the reliable ID mark. Use those pods as a study cue, not as a use decision.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Black Mustard

Brassica nigra

Season
Greens late winter; flowers spring
How to recognize it
Tall plant with lobed lower leaves, four-petal yellow flowers, slender upright seed pods.
Recognition boundary
The deck notes that black mustard is highly invasive. This page does not give removal or harvesting advice; it points to the flowers, leaves, and pods for recognition.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Wild Radish

Raphanus sativus

Season
Spring
How to recognize it
Lobed rough leaves; four-petal flowers in white, pink, or pale yellow; constricted 'beaded' seed pods.
Recognition boundary
The deck treats it as a feral garden relative. This guide keeps the rough leaves, four-petal flowers, and beaded pods as beginner study cues.

Use with care / Introduced / Herb

Sourgrass (Bermuda Buttercup)

Oxalis pes-caprae

Season
Late winter-spring
How to recognize it
Clover-like trifoliate leaves, bright five-petal yellow flowers, juicy sour stems.
Recognition boundary
The deck flags high oxalic acid and frames any taste as a tiny context note. This page does not ask you to taste, touch, or harvest it.
