Recognition-only plants to learn first

These are current deck entries marked Recognition only. Treat them as plants to know on sight, not plants to handle or test.

Recognition only / Native / Shrub

Poison Oak

Toxicodendron diversilobum

Season
Year-round; red in fall, bare canes in winter
How to recognize it
'Leaves of three,' glossy and scalloped, often reddish; grows as a shrub or climbing vine.
Deck warning
Causes a fierce urushiol rash. Even leafless winter stems trigger it — when in doubt, don't touch.

Recognition only / Introduced / Herb

Poison Hemlock

Conium maculatum

Season
Spring–summer
How to recognize it
Smooth hairless stems with purple/maroon blotches, fern-like leaves, white flower umbels, musty mousy smell.
Deck warning
DEADLY. Mistaken for fennel or wild carrot — the purple-blotched stems and lack of anise scent give it away. All parts are lethal.

Recognition only / Native / Herb

Sacred Datura (Jimsonweed)

Datura wrightii

Season
Summer–fall
How to recognize it
Sprawling gray-green plant, large soft leaves, huge white trumpet flowers (often opening at dusk), spiny round seed pods.
Deck warning
DEADLY toxic and powerfully hallucinogenic — every part. No safe dose. Do not handle and ingest.

Recognition only / Introduced / Shrub

Oleander

Nerium oleander

Season
Flowers spring–fall
How to recognize it
Large evergreen shrub, long leathery lance leaves in whorls of three, clusters of pink/white/red flowers.
Deck warning
DEADLY. Among the most poisonous common landscaping plants — never chew leaves or use stems as skewers. Smoke from burning it is toxic too.

Recognition only / Introduced / Herb

Foxglove

Digitalis purpurea

Season
Flowers late spring–summer
How to recognize it
Tall spike of drooping tubular purple/pink/white flowers spotted inside; soft fuzzy basal leaves.
Deck warning
Highly toxic to the heart. The fuzzy leaves are sometimes mistaken for comfrey — never eat them.

Recognition only / Introduced / Shrub

Castor Bean

Ricinus communis

Season
Year-round; seeds late summer–fall
How to recognize it
Tall fast-growing weedy shrub, large glossy star-shaped leaves often red-tinged, spiky red-green seed capsules with mottled bean-like seeds.
Deck warning
Extremely poisonous — the seeds especially. Never ingest any part. Included only so you can recognize and avoid it.

Recognition only / Introduced / Tree

English Yew

Taxus baccata

Season
Red 'berries' fall
How to recognize it
Dense evergreen hedge/tree, flat dark-green needles, peeling red-brown bark, soft red cup-shaped 'berries' (arils) with a hard seed.
Deck warning
DEADLY. Needles, bark, and the seed inside the red aril are lethally toxic (taxine alkaloids) — a few chewed seeds or a handful of needles can kill, with no antidote. The only non-toxic part is the thin red flesh, but the deadly seed sits right inside it, so treat the whole plant as off-limits. A very common clipped hedge — never nibble.

Recognition only / Introduced / Shrub

Privet

Ligustrum lucidum

Season
Flowers summer; black berries fall–winter
How to recognize it
Ubiquitous hedge shrub/tree, glossy pointed opposite leaves, plumes of strong-smelling cream flowers, clusters of small blue-black berries.
Deck warning
Berries and leaves are toxic to people. Among the most common Bay Area hedges — learn it so you never mistake the berries for something edible.

Recognition only / Introduced / Shrub

Firethorn (Pyracantha)

Pyracantha coccinea

Season
Orange-red berries fall–winter
How to recognize it
Dense thorny evergreen hedge, small glossy leaves, masses of tiny white flowers, then sheets of orange-red berry-like pomes.
Deck warning
The seeds contain cyanogenic compounds and the raw pomes are not a safe nibble; the thorns are vicious. Recognition, not food.

Recognition only / Introduced / Shrub

Cotoneaster

Cotoneaster franchetii

Season
Red berries fall–winter
How to recognize it
Arching shrub, small oval leaves gray-felted beneath, small pinkish flowers, dense clusters of red berry-like pomes.
Deck warning
The seeds contain cyanogenic compounds; the pomes are not safely edible. An invasive Bay Area hedge — recognition only.

Recognition only / Introduced / Tree

Horse Chestnut

Aesculus hippocastanum

Season
Candles of flowers spring; conkers fall
How to recognize it
Big tree, large palmate leaves of 5–7 leaflets, upright 'candles' of white flowers, spiny green husks holding glossy brown 'conkers'.
Deck warning
Toxic — the shiny conkers look like edible sweet chestnuts but are NOT (no flattened side, husk is spiny not bristly). Never eat them.

Caution plants and look-alike traps

These entries are not a green light to harvest. They are included because the deck warning calls out preparation, plant-part, or look-alike risk.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Wild Fennel

Foeniculum vulgare

Season
Fronds spring–fall; seeds late summer
How to recognize it
Feathery thread-like leaves, solid green stems, yellow flower umbels, strong anise/licorice smell when crushed.
Deck warning
Resembles DEADLY poison hemlock — but hemlock has purple-blotched stems and a musty smell, never anise. If it doesn't smell of licorice, walk away.

Use with care / Native / Shrub

Blue Elderberry

Sambucus nigra ssp. cerulea

Season
Flowers late spring; berries summer
How to recognize it
Large shrub/small tree, compound leaves, flat-topped clusters of tiny cream flowers, then dusty-blue berry clusters.
Deck warning
Eat only the ripe blue berries and flowers, and only COOKED. Raw berries, leaves, stems and bark are toxic. Avoid red-berried elders entirely.

Use with care / Introduced / Tree

Black Locust

Robinia pseudoacacia

Season
Fragrant white flowers late spring
How to recognize it
Tall tree, compound leaves of oval leaflets, paired thorns at the leaf base, drooping clusters of fragrant white pea-flowers, flat pods.
Deck warning
ONLY the flowers are edible (cooked). The bark, leaves, seeds and pods are toxic — never eat them. Don't confuse with other pea-family trees.

Use with care / Native / Tree

Coast Live Oak

Quercus agrifolia

Season
Acorns drop in fall
How to recognize it
Evergreen; small cupped holly-like leaves with spiny edges; gray bark; broad rounded crown.
Deck warning
Acorns are bitter with tannins and must be ground and leached before eating — never raw in quantity.

Valley Oak

Quercus lobata

Season
Acorns drop in fall
How to recognize it
Massive deciduous oak, deeply lobed leaves, checkered gray bark, long pointed acorns.
Deck warning
Like all acorns, leach the tannins before eating.

Use with care / Introduced / Tree

Blue Gum Eucalyptus

Eucalyptus globulus

Season
Year-round; flowers winter–spring
How to recognize it
Towering tree, peeling ribbon bark, sickle-shaped blue-green adult leaves with a strong menthol smell, woody button gumnuts.
Deck warning
Aromatic and medicinal, NOT a food — eucalyptus oil is toxic if ingested in quantity. Use leaves only as a steam inhalant or very weak tea.
