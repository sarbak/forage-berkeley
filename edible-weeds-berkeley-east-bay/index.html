5 deck entries

Sidewalk, lawn, and hard-packed edges

Common deck entries learners tend to notice in paths, parking strips, lawns, and disturbed ground.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Dandelion

Taraxacum officinale

Season
Year-round; best young in spring
How to recognize it
Basal rosette of jagged 'lion's tooth' leaves, single hollow stem with milky sap, yellow composite flower, puffball seed head.
Recognition boundary
The deck warns that sprayed lawns, roadsides, and yellow composite look-alikes change the context. Use the hollow stem, milky sap, rosette, and puffball seed head for recognition practice.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Common Mallow (Cheeseweed)

Malva neglecta

Season
Spring–fall
How to recognize it
Round scalloped leaves with a crinkled edge, small pale 5-petal flowers, flat 'cheese-wheel' seed pods.
Recognition boundary
The deck flags contaminated soil as the caution context. Use the round scalloped leaves, pale flowers, and cheese-wheel seed pods for recognition practice.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Broadleaf Plantain

Plantago major

Season
Spring–fall
How to recognize it
Basal rosette of broad oval leaves with prominent parallel veins; tall slender seed spikes.
Recognition boundary
The deck does not flag a dangerous local look-alike, but this page is still recognition practice, not clearance to use a real plant.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Common Sow Thistle

Sonchus oleraceus

Season
Year-round
How to recognize it
Soft (not truly spiny) jagged leaves clasping a hollow stem, milky sap, small dandelion-like yellow flowers.
Recognition boundary
Edges prickle but aren't dangerous; just a milder relative of dandelion.

Edible / Native / Herb

Pineapple Weed

Matricaria discoidea

Season
Spring–summer
How to recognize it
Feathery leaves; cone-shaped greenish-yellow flower heads with no petals; pineapple scent when crushed.
Recognition boundary
The deck highlights the pineapple scent and petal-less chamomile form. Treat scent as deck context, not a handling instruction from this guide.

6 deck entries

Cool-season garden weeds

Fast-growing herbs that show up in beds, lots, and spring growth around Berkeley.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Chickweed

Stellaria media

Season
Cool season; fall through spring
How to recognize it
Low sprawling mats, small opposite egg-shaped leaves, tiny white star flowers, single line of hairs running along the stem.
Recognition boundary
Confirm the single hairline and star flower to avoid spurges (which have milky sap and are toxic).
Species page Open photo guide

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Common Purslane

Portulaca oleracea

Season
Warm season; summer
How to recognize it
Fleshy paddle-shaped leaves, smooth reddish stems, low spreading mat, tiny yellow flowers.
Recognition boundary
Distinguish from toxic prostrate spurge, which has thin leaves and milky sap; purslane has thick juicy leaves and no milky sap.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Lamb's Quarters

Chenopodium album

Season
Spring–summer
How to recognize it
Diamond/goosefoot-shaped leaves with a white mealy coating on new growth, upright stem often striped pink.
Recognition boundary
The deck flags oxalates and toxic nightshade seedlings as the caution context. Use the white mealy new growth and goosefoot-shaped leaves for recognition practice.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Shepherd's Purse

Capsella bursa-pastoris

Season
Cool season
How to recognize it
Basal rosette of toothed leaves; thin stalk of distinctive heart/purse-shaped seed pods.
Recognition boundary
The deck points to the purse-shaped pods as the reliable field mark. Treat the timing notes as deck context, not use advice.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Black Mustard

Brassica nigra

Season
Greens late winter; flowers spring
How to recognize it
Tall plant with lobed lower leaves, four-petal yellow flowers, slender upright seed pods.
Recognition boundary
The deck flags this as highly invasive. Learn the four-petal yellow flowers and upright seed pods without treating this guide as harvesting advice.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Wild Radish

Raphanus sativus

Season
Spring
How to recognize it
Lobed rough leaves; four-petal flowers in white/pink/pale yellow; constricted 'beaded' seed pods.
Recognition boundary
The deck treats this as a feral garden relative, but the guide still does not clear any real plant for use. Practice the rough lobed leaves, pale four-petal flowers, and beaded pods.

4 deck entries

Moist shade and trail-edge plants

Plants from the deck that need extra attention because habitat, stings, or toxic look-alikes can change the learning context.

Edible / Native / Herb

Miner's Lettuce

Claytonia perfoliata

Season
Late winter–spring
How to recognize it
Round disc-like leaf with the flower stem passing straight through its center; tiny white flowers.
Recognition boundary
The deck points to the perfoliate disc leaf as the main recognition cue. Use that field mark for practice, not as a safety shortcut.

Use with care / Native / Herb

Stinging Nettle

Urtica dioica

Season
Spring
How to recognize it
Opposite toothed leaves, square stems, fine stinging hairs along stems and leaf edges.
Recognition boundary
The deck flags raw stings. Treat that as a handling boundary and use this page for recognition practice only.
Species page Open photo guide

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Three-cornered Leek

Allium triquetrum

Season
Late winter–spring
How to recognize it
Distinctly three-sided (triangular) flower stem, drooping white bell flowers with a green stripe, strong onion/garlic smell.
Recognition boundary
The deck's allium rule is strict: a true onion or garlic smell matters, and no smell means no allium assumption. Do not handle plants to test that from this page.

Edible / Introduced / Herb

Wild Fennel

Foeniculum vulgare

Season
Fronds spring–fall; seeds late summer
How to recognize it
Feathery thread-like leaves, solid green stems, yellow flower umbels, strong anise/licorice smell when crushed.
Recognition boundary
The deck flags deadly Poison Hemlock as the look-alike risk. Learn the green stems, yellow umbels, and anise-scent contrast without handling plants to test them.
