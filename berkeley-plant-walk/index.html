Forage Berkeley is a free photo-practice companion for people learning plants around Berkeley and the East Bay. It can help you recognize common shapes and toxic look-alikes, but it does not replace an expert-led class or give foraging permission.

How to use it

Keep the walk route simple

Pick a familiar sidewalk, garden edge, or public trail where observation is allowed. Look, compare, and practice later from photos. Do not collect plants, test smells by handling them, or treat the app as a field guide for use decisions.

Good fit

Use this before a class to learn names, after a guided walk to review, or on an ordinary neighborhood walk to notice recurring plant shapes.

Not a class

Forage Berkeley does not certify IDs, teach harvesting, or imply permission to forage in parks or other managed spaces.

Route-agnostic prompts

Practice what you can see from the path

These prompts work without naming a route or asking you to touch anything.

Leaf rosettes

Look for low circles of leaves near compacted ground. Compare jagged, round, and broad-veined leaves in the app later.

Yellow flowers

Notice whether the plant has one composite flower head, four mustard-family petals, or a clover-like leaf below the bloom.

Look-alikes first

Review plants marked recognition-only before you go. If a plant may be a toxic look-alike, stop at observation.
